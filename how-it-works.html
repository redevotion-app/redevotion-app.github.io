How it works

Redevotion keeps a Scripture-centered devotion for you and for your household: a Personal Devotion and Family Worship, each an order of parts you choose, drawing on the practices you keep and the church’s historic texts. Here is how it behaves, part by part, and where everything you add is kept.

The shape of the app

Redevotion has three tabs. Today gathers what is set for the day: Personal Devotion and Family Worship at the top, then the practices you keep, such as Bible Reading, Catechism and Prayer. Home shows each practice with its progress and its settings, and your notes. Settings holds the rest.

You choose what appears. Each practice and each devotion has its own switch; only what you turn on is shown.

The practices

A practice keeps one kind of devotional work and its progress. Each can be used on its own from Today, or become a part of a devotion.

Bible Reading

Choose a plan: Robert Murray M’Cheyne’s 365 readings, the whole Bible, the New Testament, the Old Testament, Psalms and Proverbs, or a plan you build yourself. M’Cheyne’s plan keeps its Family and Private passages, so the Family passages can be read in family worship and the Private ones on your own.

The day’s readings are given as references, and each passage is checked off on its own. Any passage opens in the Bible app or website you prefer.

Catechism

Four catechisms: the Westminster Shorter Catechism, the Baptist Catechism, the Catechism for Young Children, and A Catechism for Boys and Girls. Questions come in order, a few at a time.

Answers stay hidden until you tap Show Answer, so you can answer first; the setting is on to begin with and can be turned off. The Westminster Shorter Catechism, Baptist Catechism, and Catechism for Young Children offer Modern and Traditional readings. Scripture proofs are included with the Westminster Shorter Catechism, Baptist Catechism, and A Catechism for Boys and Girls.

Prayer

Keep the people and requests you pray for, in categories you name, such as Family and Friends. Each day Redevotion brings some of them forward in a gentle rotation, so that over time everyone is prayed for without the whole list falling on one day. Mark each as you pray for them.

Memory

Memory holds what you are learning by heart: Scripture, catechism answers, creeds and prayers. It is reviewed as a part of a devotion, so it sits inside the practice you already keep. In Family Worship, Family Memory keeps the household’s memory work apart from your own.

Personal Devotion and Family Worship

A devotion is an order of parts you choose: Scripture for a call to worship, historic prayers of adoration or confession, a creed, the catechism, your Bible reading, prayer and memory, and a benediction. Each part opens on its own reading surface, with the passage itself in the translation you choose.

Personal Devotion

Your own order of parts, kept for you alone. It can be as short as a single part or as full as you like: a call to worship, the day’s reading and a benediction make a simple beginning. Add, remove and reorder parts whenever you like; none is required.

Family Worship

Family Worship keeps the household’s own order, separate from yours. One person leads it from one phone. Its prayer and memory parts, Family Prayer and Family Memory, keep the household’s requests and memory work apart from each person’s own.

How a devotion moves

The same rules hold in both, and they are worth knowing, because nothing is ever recorded for you.

Begin
Opens the first part you haven’t finished.
Completing
You mark each passage, question or part complete yourself. Opening or scrolling a part never does. Completing a part moves you to the next; completing the last finishes the devotion.
Continue
Goes to the next unfinished part. It records nothing.
Contents
Shows every part, which are done, and where you are.
Finish for Now
Closes the devotion. What you completed stays completed; the rest waits.
Resume
Reopens a devotion you finished for now, where you left off.
Revisit
Reopens a finished devotion, to read it again.

A part drawn from the Library moves through its collection the same way. When you complete it, the next devotion brings the next text; if you didn’t, the same one waits for you. Nothing is counted as missed.

The Library

Redevotion carries a library of the church’s historic texts, read on your device: the Westminster Confession and the Second London Baptist Confession, the ecumenical creeds, the four catechisms, thirty-six historic prayers, and Scripture collections arranged for worship, such as Call to Worship, Prayer for Illumination and Assurance of Pardon.

The historic prayers and the confessions can be read in a Modern or a Traditional reading. Every text keeps its source, and a shorter selection opens to the full text it comes from. From any devotion part, View in Library opens the whole.

Scripture

Seven translations: ESV, KJV, CSB, NASB 1995, NASB 2020, LSB and NKJV. The LSB is the default, and you can choose another in Settings.

The Scripture text of the passages used in devotion parts is built into the app, within each publisher’s permissions. Bible Reading plans give references, and any passage can also be opened in the Bible app or website you already use. Each translation’s copyright notice is in Settings, under About & Legal, Scripture Copyrights.

Making it yours

Almost everything can be arranged, and nothing has to be. These are the choices, and where they live.

What appears
Each practice and each devotion has its own switch. Only what you turn on is shown.
The order
Add, remove and reorder the parts of each devotion. None is required; a devotion needs only one part.
Each part
A part drawn from the Library can move through all its texts in order, only through the ones you choose, or keep always the same one. Where a part offers a choice of source, it can draw on Scripture, historic prayers, or both; a historic prayer can be the shorter selection, where one is given, or the full prayer.
Wording
Historic prayers, confessions and three of the catechisms can be read in Modern or Traditional wording.
Scripture
Choose one of the seven translations, and the Bible app or website your references open in.
Answers
Catechism answers stay hidden until tapped; the setting can be turned off.
Reminders
Set a reminder for a reading plan or a prayer category, at the time you choose. Reminders are scheduled on your phone.

Ways to use Redevotion shows seven arrangements, with how to set each up.

Your data

Everything you add stays on your device. There is no account to create, and there are no ads and no tracking. If you turn reminders on, they are scheduled on your phone.

Export a backup file whenever you like and save it where you choose; restore it on the same phone or a new one. Before a restore replaces anything, Redevotion keeps a safety copy of what was there. The privacy policy sets this out in full.
