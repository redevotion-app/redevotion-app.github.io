Ways to use Redevotion

The same few parts serve very different lives. Here are seven ways to arrange them: who each suits, what it uses, how to set it up, and why it may help. Each links to how those parts work.

  1. Beginning a daily devotion
  2. Bringing the routine you already keep

  5. Praying with the church’s historic prayers

Beginning a daily devotion

Begin small, with three parts: a call to worship from Scripture, your Bible reading, and a benediction. That is enough to open the Bible, read it, and close in blessing. Keep it at three until it is part of the day, and add only what you want. If a day goes unread, the parts you didn’t finish are there the next time.

For
Someone who wants a daily practice and has none yet, or has tried and stopped.
Uses
Personal Devotion; Bible Reading; Today.
To set it up
  1. Turn on Personal Devotion and Bible Reading.
  2. Choose a reading plan. Psalms and Proverbs is a lighter start; M’Cheyne’s reads the whole Bible in a year.
  3. Begin with three parts: a call to worship, your reading, and a benediction. Begin from Today.

Bringing the routine you already keep

Keep your plan, and keep your Bible app. Redevotion gathers the routine you already have rather than replacing it: the same plan, read in the Bible you already use, with the people you pray for kept beside it and brought forward a few at a time.

For
A reader who already follows M’Cheyne or another plan, and keeps a prayer list on paper.
Uses
Bible Reading and its plans; Prayer, in your own categories; your preferred Bible app or website.
To set it up
  1. In Bible Reading, choose the plan you already follow.
  2. Choose the Bible app or website you already use. Each reference opens there.
  3. In Prayer, make categories of your own and move your list into them. Its rotation brings some forward each day.
  4. Decide how they sit together: each on its own from Today, or as parts of a Personal Devotion, so the reading and the praying happen in one order.

Leading family worship

Family Worship keeps the household’s order apart from your own, and one person leads it from one phone. A gathering can be short, or a longer order of the kind shown on the home page. When it is cut short, what you finished stays finished, and the rest is there next time: nothing to catch up on.

For
Parents leading worship at home, with children of any age.
Uses
Family Worship; Family Prayer and Family Memory; M’Cheyne’s Family passages; Catechism; Finish for Now.
To set it up
  1. Turn on Family Worship.
  2. Set its order. A short one: a call to worship, the day’s Family passages from M’Cheyne’s plan, a catechism question, and Family Prayer.
  3. Keep the household’s requests in Family Prayer and its memory work in Family Memory, apart from each person’s own.
  4. Lead it from Today. If the gathering ends early, choose Finish for Now.

Learning a catechism, and Scripture by heart

A catechism is learned a question at a time. Redevotion keeps the place, brings the next questions in order, and hides each answer until you tap, so you can say it before you see it. Memory work sits inside the devotion you already keep, so review becomes part of the day rather than something kept elsewhere.

For
A person or a household beginning a catechism, or adding memory work to a practice already kept.
Uses
Catechism (four catechisms, with Modern or Traditional wording and Scripture proofs where the catechism has them); Memory.
To set it up
  1. Turn on Catechism and choose one. The Catechism for Young Children and A Catechism for Boys and Girls suit younger households; the Westminster Shorter and the Baptist Catechism suit older children and adults.
  2. Leave Hide Answers Until Tapped on, as it is to begin with, to answer before you look.
  3. Add Catechism, Memory, or both as parts of a devotion.

Praying with the church’s historic prayers

A part of historic prayer moves through its collection one prayer at a time, and only moves on after you have completed the prayer it gave you. Each prayer keeps its author and source, and a shorter selection opens to the whole prayer, so you always know where the words come from.

For
Someone drawn to written prayers who isn’t sure where to begin.
Uses
Library parts such as Adoration and Confession; Modern and Traditional readings; View in Library.
To set it up
  1. Add a part of historic prayer, such as Adoration or Confession, to a devotion.
  2. In the part’s settings, choose how it moves: through all its prayers in order, only through the ones you choose, or always the same one.
  3. Choose the shorter selection, where one is given, or the full prayer.
  4. Choose the Modern reading for plainer present-day wording, or the Traditional reading for the older wording of the source.

Through changing seasons

An order is meant to change. Shorten it for a season, down to a single reading if need be, and add parts back when you are ready. Unfinished parts wait rather than piling up as missed, so a hard month leaves no backlog.

For
A new parent, a shift worker, a student at exam time, anyone ill or grieving.
Uses
Adding, removing and reordering parts; Finish for Now.
To set it up
  1. Remove the parts you can’t keep for now. A devotion needs only one.
  2. On days that end early, choose Finish for Now: what you did stays done.
  3. When the season turns, add parts back, one at a time.

When you want more

Once an order is settled, there is more to use, each when it helps and not before: the Library, read on its own; a part drawn from Scripture, from historic prayers, or both; reminders at a time you choose; notes; and a backup file to carry everything to a new phone.

For
Someone who has kept an order for a while and wants it to fit more closely.
Uses
The Library; each part’s source and selection; reminders; Notes; backup and restore.
To set it up
  1. Open the Library on its own to read a confession, a creed or a prayer in full.
  2. In a part’s settings, choose its source where it offers one: Scripture, historic prayers, or both.
  3. Set a reminder for a reading plan or a prayer category, at the time you choose. Reminders are scheduled on your phone.
  4. Export a backup file now and then, and keep it where you choose.
