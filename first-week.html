Your first week

A first week, if you’d like one. Nothing here is a program to complete. It is one sensible way to begin, a little at a time, and you can leave it whenever it stops helping.

The first day

Begin with very little. Choose the practices you mean to keep, starting with Bible reading, and let the first devotion be short: a passage that calls you to worship, the day’s reading, and a word of blessing to close is enough.

Pick a reading plan and the translation you prefer, and read. Nothing else needs to be set up on the first day; what you leave for later will wait.

Getting to know it

In the first few days, notice how a devotion moves. Begin opens the first part you haven’t finished. You mark each part complete yourself; opening or scrolling it never does. Continue goes on to the next part without recording anything, and Contents shows every part and where you are.

That is most of what there is to learn. The rest can wait until you want it. How a devotion moves

The days after

Add one thing at a time, if you want to: a prayer list, then a catechism, then a part from the Library. Keep each for a few days before adding the next, and leave out anything that crowds the order.

If you lead a household

When you are ready, begin Family Worship, and keep it short at first. One phone leads, and the household’s order is kept apart from your own. How Family Worship works

If a day is missed

Nothing needs repairing. Unfinished parts wait for you, and nothing is counted as missed. If a devotion is interrupted, Finish for Now keeps what you completed.

After the first week

By now the order is yours. When you want to shape it further, for a household, a catechism or a different season, the ways to use Redevotion show how.
